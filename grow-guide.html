Grow Guide

Growing your live Spirulina

You've got a living culture, not a dried powder. Treat it like a small, easy pet: keep it warm, bright and fed, and it will grow fresh spirulina for you for months. Here's everything from unboxing to your first harvest.

  • Warmth

    25–35 °C

  • Light

    4–6 h / day

  • Water

    pH ~9–10

  • First harvest

    ~2–3 wks

01

What's in your pack

Your Small Starter Pack has three things: a live, concentrated Spirulina culture, a sachet of dry nutrient that makes the special water spirulina lives in, and this guide. Spirulina (Arthrospira platensis) isn't a plant — it's a microscopic blue-green algae that floats in warm, mineral-rich, slightly alkaline water and multiplies in the light.

That's the whole idea: you'll mix the nutrient into clean water to make its home, pour in the living culture to seed it, and let it grow until it's dense enough to harvest.

02

Set it up

About ten minutes, best done the day it arrives. You'll need a clear glass or food-grade plastic container (a wide jar or small tank is ideal — more surface means more light and air).

  1. Let it settle

    Stand the pouch upright and let it rest for about 30 minutes after the journey. Starting today? Keep it at room temperature. Not starting for a day or two? Pop it in the fridge until you're ready.

  2. Prepare the water

    Fill your container with dechlorinated or filtered water — chlorine from the tap harms the culture. Tap water left to stand overnight works perfectly. Room temperature, not cold.

  3. Add the nutrient

    Stir in the dry nutrient at the dose printed on your pack card. This turns plain water into the warm, alkaline, mineral home spirulina needs. Mix until it's dissolved.

  4. Pour in the culture

    Add your live concentrate and stir gently. It'll look thin and pale green at first — completely normal for a fresh start. It hasn't failed; it's just about to begin.

  5. Find its spot

    A warm, bright place out of harsh direct midday sun — a windowsill or near a grow light is perfect. Cover it loosely: it needs to breathe, so never seal it tight.

03

Daily care

Barely a minute a day. The culture does the work; you just keep the conditions right.

  • Stir once or twice a day. Thirty seconds keeps the spirulina suspended and exchanging gas with the air, instead of settling at the bottom.

  • Keep it warm and bright. Cooler or darker just means slower growth, not death — so don't panic if your room dips.

  • Top up as it evaporates. Add a splash of dechlorinated water now and then to keep the level steady.

04

Watching it grow

Over the first days to weeks it deepens from pale to rich green and gets harder to see through — that's the spirulina multiplying. Dip a clean teaspoon in: when you can only just make it out a few centimetres down, the culture is getting dense.

A healthy culture smells earthy and grassy, looks a fresh vivid green, and swirls smoothly when stirred. Your first proper harvest usually comes around two to three weeks in — sooner when it's warm and bright, later when it's cool.

05

Harvesting

When it's thick and deep green, you can take some.

  • Pour a portion through a fine cloth or mesh (around 30–50 µm). The green paste left behind is your fresh spirulina.

  • Rinse it lightly and use it the same day, or keep it in the fridge for 2–3 days, or freeze it in small portions.

  • Never harvest it all. Leave at least half the culture behind so it can regrow — this is the difference between a one-off and a supply that lasts.

06

Keeping it going

After harvesting, pour the strained water back into the container, top it up with fresh dechlorinated water, and feed a little more nutrient (per your pack card) to replace what the crop took out. Then it's back to the simple rhythm: feed, light, warmth, stir.

Kept this way, one culture lives and produces for months. You're not buying spirulina — you're keeping it.

07

Troubleshooting

Pale, or not thickening

Almost always too cool or too dark. Move it somewhere warmer and brighter, keep feeding, and give it time — slow is normal, not a failure.

Going yellow or brown, or smelling bad

The culture is stressed or crashing — usually too hot (above ~38 °C), too little light, or overfed. Cool it down, dilute with fresh prepared water, and ease off the nutrient.

Clumping or settling at the bottom

It wants more movement. Stir more often; a healthy culture stays suspended through the water.

A little foam or surface film

Usually harmless. Skim it off and keep the water gently moving.

It looks still after shipping

Give it a few warm, bright days before worrying. Cultures routinely bounce back from the quiet of the post.
